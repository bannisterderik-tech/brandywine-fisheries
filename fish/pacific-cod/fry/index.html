Frying

Frying Pacific cod

350–375°F oil · 3–5 minutes depending on thickness, until deep golden. Pull at 135°F.

Call (541) 505-9686Where to find us
To fry Pacific cod: 350–375°F oil, cook to 135°F, roughly 3–5 minutes depending on thickness, until deep golden. Pacific Cod is very lean, which means there is very little margin — use a thermometer. The mistake to avoid: crowding the pot. It is the single most common frying error and it makes everything soggy.

The method

  1. Set the heat350–375°F oil. A heavy pot filled no more than a third, a clip thermometer, a wire rack.
  2. Prepare the fishDry the fish, then flour, then batter. Fry in small batches — every piece you add drops the oil temperature, and oil below 325°F soaks in instead of crisping. Let it recover between batches. Drain on a rack, never on paper, which steams the underside soft.
  3. Time it3–5 minutes depending on thickness, until deep golden. The classic fish-and-chips fish. The big tender flake that makes it good battered is the same thing that makes it fall apart on a grill — keep it in a pan or in oil.
  4. Check it135°F; the FDA guideline is 145°F. Golden and floating. Fish fries fast.

Why Pacific cod suits this

Lean white fish. Rockfish, cod, lingcod — this is what fish and chips is made of.

Pacific Cod is very lean with large, tender flake that breaks apart easily. The classic fish-and-chips fish. The big tender flake that makes it good battered is the same thing that makes it fall apart on a grill — keep it in a pan or in oil.

Batter. Cold beer batter, mixed at the last second and left lumpy. Overmixing develops gluten and turns the crust tough.

The mistake. Crowding the pot. It is the single most common frying error and it makes everything soggy.
