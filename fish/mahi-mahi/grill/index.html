Grilling Mahi Mahi

Direct, 450–500°F · Roughly 8–10 minutes per inch of thickness, total, turned once. Pull at 135°F.

Call (541) 505-9686Where to find us
To grill Mahi Mahi: direct, 450–500°F, cook to 135°F, roughly roughly 8–10 minutes per inch of thickness, total, turned once. Mahi Mahi is lean, which means there is very little margin — use a thermometer. The mistake to avoid: turning too early. Nearly every piece of fish that tears on a grill was moved before it had released.

The method

  1. Set the heatDirect, 450–500°F. Charcoal or gas grill, a well-scraped and oiled grate, a thin metal spatula.
  2. Prepare the fishGet the grate ripping hot and clean it twice. Oil the fish, not the grate — oil on hot bars burns before the fish lands. Put the fish down and leave it alone; it releases on its own when a crust forms, usually three to four minutes. If it sticks, it is not ready to turn.
  3. Time itRoughly 8–10 minutes per inch of thickness, total, turned once. Firm enough to grill without falling apart, which is most of its appeal. Lean enough that it wants a marinade or a sauce.
  4. Check it135°F; the FDA guideline is 145°F. Pull 5°F below your target — carryover finishes it while it rests.

Why Mahi Mahi suits this

Firm, thick, and ideally fatty. Fat drips, flares, and bastes.

Mahi Mahi is lean with firm with a large flake. Firm enough to grill without falling apart, which is most of its appeal. Lean enough that it wants a marinade or a sauce.

Skin. Skin-on, skin-side down first, and leave it there for two-thirds of the total time. The skin is a heat shield.

The mistake. Turning too early. Nearly every piece of fish that tears on a grill was moved before it had released.
