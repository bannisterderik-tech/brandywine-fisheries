Brandywine Fisheries sells never-frozen Oregon seafood from a hand-built boat working out of Charleston harbor. Their retail store at 4731 Main St #1, Springfield, OR is open daily 10am–6pm — call (541) 505-9686. They also run five farmers market days a week in Eugene (Saturday and Tuesday), Corvallis, Bend and Portland. The case runs from black mussels at $6.95 to halibut at $38.95 a pound, plus a thirteen-piece smokehouse line and their own canned albacore.

The whole argument, in one sentence

Their boat uses an ice hold instead of a freezer — which means it has to come back to the dock more often, and the fish reaches a counter in days rather than weeks.

Our boat utilizes an "ICE" fish-hold that keeps our fish fresh from the point of catch to the time the boat docks. Not freezing our catch means we dock more often and must employ various tactics that ensure we don't bruise the fish while on-board the boat.— Brandywine Fisheries, in their own words

Questions people actually ask

Where is the Brandywine Fisheries store?

4731 Main St #1, Springfield, OR 97478 — open daily, 10am to 6pm. The phone is (541) 505-9686.

Which farmers markets does Brandywine attend?

Five market days across four cities: Eugene on Saturdays 9am–3pm and Tuesdays 10am–3pm at the Park Blocks at 8th & Oak, Corvallis on Saturdays 9am–1pm at 1st & Jackson, Bend on Wednesdays 11am–3pm in Brooks Alley downtown, and Portland on Saturdays 8:30am–2pm at the PSU market on SW Park and Montgomery.

Is the fish frozen?

No. Their site is explicit about it: the boat uses an "ICE" fish-hold that keeps the catch fresh from the point of catch to the time the boat docks. As they put it, not freezing the catch means they dock more often.

What does "sushi grade" mean here?

It is not a regulated term anywhere in the United States, so it always means whatever the seller's handling practice is. Brandywine ties the phrase to a specific practice — icing at the point of catch, docking often rather than freezing, and handling that avoids bruising the fish on board.

Where does the fish come from?

The Brandywine is an Oregon boat working out of Charleston harbor on the southern Oregon coast, where it was hand-built. Some items on the list — ahi, swordfish, mahi mahi, large raw shrimp and scallops — are not Oregon fisheries and are sourced rather than landed by the boat. Those are labelled as such throughout this site.

Can I order ahead for a farmers market?

Yes — there is a market pre-order list covering the fresh fish and most of the smoked line, including half-pound portions. It matters most at the short markets: Corvallis and Bend both run four hours, and popular cuts go early.

What is the most expensive fish you sell?

Halibut, at $38.95 a pound — more than chinook salmon at $29.95. The single most expensive item overall is the Smoked Seacuterie Board at $229.95.

What is the cheapest thing you sell?

Black mussels at $6.95. Bay shrimp are $9.00, mahi mahi $9.95, and Pacific rockfish $13.95 a pound — the least expensive fresh fillet.
