About the black cod (Sablefish)

Flavor. Rich and buttery, closer to a fatty salmon than to a white fish. Texture. Silky, unusually large soft flake that separates into velvety sheets. Fat. Very high — the highest oil content of anything in the case.

All that oil makes black cod nearly impossible to overcook. It is the forgiving fish — if halibut intimidates you, start here.

Where it comes from. Longline and pot gear, fished deep, landed at Charleston, Oregon. Fished essentially year-round under a quota system rather than in a short seasonal window.

Buying it. Look for a bright, almost wet-looking sheen. Dull black cod has been sitting.

Keeping it. The high oil content means it holds a little better than lean fish, but two to three days is still the window.

Questions

Is black cod (Sablefish) available all year in Corvallis?

Fished essentially year-round under a quota system rather than in a short seasonal window. This market is seasonal — The riverfront Saturday market season runs mid-April through late November. Seasons shift annually, so call (541) 505-9686 first.

Can I pre-order black cod (Sablefish) for Corvallis?

Yes — the market pre-order list covers the fresh case and most of the smoked line. Worth doing here, since what's on the table is finite.

How much black cod (Sablefish) should I buy?

5–6 oz per person; it is rich enough that people eat less of it. It's $18.95 / lb.

How do I get it home from Corvallis?

A cooler with ice, the fish sitting on top of the ice rather than buried in it, and a straight drive. About 45 minutes up the valley from Springfield.

How should I cook it?

All that oil makes black cod nearly impossible to overcook. It is the forgiving fish — if halibut intimidates you, start here. Target 140°F — this is the one fish that genuinely wants to go a little further.
